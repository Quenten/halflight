# World Primer — Cinderreach, Vesper System

## The system

Humanity's first extrasolar foothold: the **Vesper system**, four decades of
colonization deep. What was sold as the new frontier became a ledger entry.
Charter corporations own the orbits, syndicates own the gaps between them, and
the factions that came out here for ideology now fight over territory like
everyone else. Traffic runs constantly between the orbital yards, the relay
stations, and the surface — freighters, shuttle stacks, salvage tugs, and the
occasional corporate frigate reminding everyone whose charter it is.

The prize and the problem is **Vesper III** — the only marginally habitable world.
Its terraforming spine stalled twenty years ago. Officially: "phase-two
recalibration." Practically: the atmosphere processors run at a third capacity
and the planet is decaying on schedule. Nobody is evacuating. There is nowhere
cheaper to go.

## The city

**Cinderreach** is the planet's megalopolis — twelve million people stacked
vertically into a canyon system under a dusk that never lifts (Vesper III's slow
rotation keeps the habitable band in permanent halflight). The city doesn't
sprawl; it piles.

- **The Crest** — corporate spires and the landing terraces. Filtered air, real
  light, quantum-linked trading floors where colony futures change hands.
- **The Terraces** — kilometer-tall residential stacks and the market decks:
  noodle steam, chrome clinics, neon signage in six languages, crowds that never
  thin. Most of the city lives and dies here without ever seeing the Crest.
- **The Undervault** — the sealed construction galleries and service strata below.
  Syndicate territory: unlicensed body-shops, off-ledger reactors, debt markets,
  and things the corporations write off rather than retrieve.

Beyond the walls: the **Grayreach** — storm-scoured flats dotted with dead
terraformer nodes, crashed freight, and anomalous zones the survey maps mark
simply as DO NOT TRANSIT. Things live out there that the colonial biologists
stopped publishing about. Salvage crews go out anyway. The pay is real.

## Technology

Old and new welded together — nothing matches, everything is maintained past its
rated life:

- **Chrome and nanoweave.** Cybernetic limbs, ocular suites, and subdermal
  nanomachine therapies are commodities — corporate-grade on the Crest,
  refurbished and unlicensed below. Every augment carries a ledger tag; the
  Undervault's business is removing them.
- **Quantumtech.** Instant system-wide comms and encrypted corporate networks
  for those who can pay. Everyone else queues for relay time.
- **Walkers.** Heavy industrial exoframes and armored combat walkers — mining
  rigs retrofitted with autocannon. Syndicates field them in territory disputes;
  Watch Command fields them back.
- **Starships.** Built and rebuilt at the orbital yards. Engineers who can keep
  a drive core certified never lack work — or people trying to own them.
- **The old stack.** Beneath all of it: analog switchgear, pneumatic doors,
  CRT terminals, paper manifests. When the storms cut power, the old stack is
  what still works.

## Powers

- **The Meridian Combine** — the charter corporation. Owns the air ration, the
  water, the ledger every colonist is born into, and the orbital yards. Not
  cruel by policy — cruel by accounting.
- **The Saltline** — the dominant syndicate. Off-ledger salvage, chrome without
  tags, debt, quiet passage offworld. Brutal creditors, reliable partners.
  In the Undervault their word outranks the charter.
- **Watch Command** — Combine security. Underpaid, negotiable, until you make
  the Watchlist. Then it stops being negotiable.
- **The lesser houses** — rival corps, colonist blocs, and splinter factions
  fighting over territory the Combine can no longer afford to hold. The map of
  Cinderreach is redrawn quarter by quarter, deck by deck.

## Currency & stakes

**Scrip** — Combine ledger credit, priced against colony futures on the Crest
exchange. Everything costs scrip: air-ration top-ups, clinic time, augment
licenses, dock fees. Running out of scrip in Cinderreach is not an
inconvenience. It is a countdown.

## Tone anchors

- The frontier didn't fail loudly. It just stopped being maintained.
- Chrome is common; being whole is the luxury.
- Everyone is one bad shift from the Undervault, and knows it.
- The halflight never changes. People do the changing.
